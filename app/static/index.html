The pipeline

Four stages, one ranked list

Your plain-language query flows through four stages before returning a ranked list of restaurants, each with the review sentences that explain the match.
01
Query Rewriting

An LLM converts vague or negated language into reviewer-style keywords before anything is encoded — so "no waiting line" becomes "quick seating walk-in no queue".

Groq / Qwen3-27B
02
Bi-encoder Retrieval

240,660 pre-encoded review sentences are ranked by cosine similarity against the rewritten query embedding. Multilingual — works in both English and Thai.

mpnet-multilingual
03
Score Aggregation

Each restaurant's score is the mean of its top-10 sentence scores — a simple and volume-neutral aggregation that consistently outperforms restaurant-level pooling.

Top-10 mean
04
Cross-encoder Re-rank

Activated only for negation queries. Jointly re-scores (query, sentence) pairs to filter semantic mismatches that the bi-encoder misses on negated phrasing.

ms-marco-MiniLM
Why it's different

Three things that set
TasteFinder apart

01 —

Sentence-level evidence

Every ranked restaurant comes with the actual review sentences that matched your query — not a star average, not a category tag. You can read exactly why a place was recommended.

02 —

Understands what you mean

An LLM rewrites vague or negated queries before retrieval. "I don't want a long wait" becomes search terms reviewers actually use, so the encoder finds the right sentences instead of matching the wrong ones.

03 —

English and Thai

The same multilingual encoder handles both languages natively. The English track is benchmarked on RIRD with five-annotator binary relevance; Thai is evaluated on Wongnai reviews with hand-annotated taste queries.

Try it with your own query

The search runs on 100 pre-computed RIRD queries (instant) and accepts any new English query through the live pipeline. Thai queries use 24 frozen Wongnai results.

Explanation evaluation · B1

Automatic faithfulness test

We tested whether deleting the displayed sentences hurts ranking more than deleting an equal number of random sentences. The mean faithfulness gap was exactly 0.000 (p = 1.0, n = 300 query–restaurant pairs).

Both deletion conditions reduced nDCG@10 from 0.682 to 0.379. The test is inconclusive: each restaurant's evidence pool is only 10 sentences, making random and shown deletions indistinguishable by design. A larger candidate pool per restaurant would be needed for a meaningful test.

Explanation evaluation · B2

Human support rating

375 items (25 queries × 3 restaurants × 5 sentences) were labelled 0/1/2 by two annotators. 60.5% of sentences were rated as at least partially supporting, and 22.4% as clearly supporting. Inter-annotator agreement on the 40-pair overlap was κ = 0.544 (moderate), exact agreement 70%.

Per-query rates vary widely. Queries with direct positive phrasing reached 100% support; negation-heavy queries such as "no waiting line" and "outdoor space without live music" received 0% — confirming the negation blindness that B3 was designed to address.
